# Six AI Archetypes deck: claims checked against the NovaMart traces

_Generated by `backend/scripts/validate_deck_claims.py` from the Category 1 experiment (`backend/experiments/cat1_ablation/runs.jsonl` + `backend/replays/cat1_ablation_summary.json`), the Category 2 experiment (`backend/experiments/cat2_not_stated/drafts.jsonl` + `backend/replays/cat2_quality_summary.json`, section regeneration from `backend/experiments/cat2_not_stated/section_regens.jsonl`), the Category 3 quality rerun (`backend/experiments/cat3_quality/runs.jsonl` + `backend/replays/cat3_quality_summary.json`), the Category 4 quality rerun (`backend/experiments/cat4_quality/runs.jsonl` + `backend/replays/cat4_quality_summary.json`), the Category 5 ablation (`backend/experiments/cat5_ablation/runs.jsonl` + `backend/replays/cat5_ablation_summary.json`), the Category 6 fair rerun (`backend/experiments/cat6_fair_rerun/runs.jsonl`) and `backend/traces/`. Re-run the script after new runs._

These experiments compare system designs (what is asked for, how much context is sent, caching, tools, routing between model sizes) in one synthetic lab setup; the results describe those configurations on these tasks and are not an assessment of any model.

## Trace store

`backend/traces/` holds 208 trace files (live_captured_seed 208), 208 distinct runs of the current golden replays (cat1_classification_routing 48 files / 48 runs; cat2_synthesis_drafting 48 files / 48 runs; cat3_conversational_assistants 70 files / 70 runs; cat4_multidoc_rag 24 files / 24 runs; cat5_process_orchestration 9 files / 9 runs; cat6_multi_agent_swarms 9 files / 9 runs). The figures below are computed from the experiment records and golden replays, not from the traces. Replay requests do not write traces; `_backups/` is not read.

**Unit of analysis:** each category's experiment records; the golden replays the app serves are repeat 0 of each cell. Category 1 uses all 144 experiment runs (4 cells × 12 items × 3 repeats, n=36 per cell); its golden replays are repeat 0 of each cell. Category 2 uses the latest ok experiment draft per document × model × version × repeat (48 of 48 planned, n=12 per cell); its golden replays are repeat 0 of each cell. Category 3 uses the 12 quality-rerun records of its without/with design (2 versions × 2 sessions × 3 repeats, n=6 per version); its golden replays are repeat 0 of both. Category 4 uses the 12 quality-rerun sessions of its without/with design (2 versions × 2 corpus sizes × 3 sessions of 8 questions, 96 answers, n=24 per row); its golden replays are repeat 0 of both versions at the 7-document corpus. Category 5 uses the 144 recorded runs of its with/without design on Sonnet and Haiku (4 cells × 12 invoices × 3 repeats, n=36 per cell); its golden replays are repeat 0 of each cell on inv-01..03. Category 6 uses the 45 fair-rerun records of its with/without design (3 cells × 3 incidents × 5 repeats, n=15 per cell); its golden replays are repeat 0 of each cell. Costs are recomputed per API call from recorded token counts, priced by the model that served the call. The traces' own `finops` blocks are not used, so the results don't depend on how the app priced them.

**Cost views.** *Billed*: cache tokens exactly as the API reported them during capture. *Warm*: every cached-prefix token is a cache read (steady state). *Cold*: the first call per model in each unit writes the prefix. All dollar figures use Anthropic's published rates (the rate card in `backend/app/core/pricing.py`).

### 1. Classification, extraction & routing (per 1k items, n=36 per cell)

Source: the Category 1 experiment, 144 runs (4 cells × 12 items × 3 repeats) from `backend/experiments/cat1_ablation/runs.jsonl`, selected by the run ids in `backend/replays/cat1_ablation_summary.json`; quality read from the same file. \* The write-up prompt never lists the allowed labels. For the write-ups, "fields correct" counts the fields where the reader model could read one allowed label off the write-up and it was right: whether the write-up is usable as a classification, not the rest of its content. 44 (Sonnet) and 44 (Haiku) of 126 fields were `ambiguous` (several answers, or wording that maps to no single label, e.g. "Status: HOLD / DO NOT PUBLISH. Route to Compliance Audit."). Ground truth covers 42 fields per repeat (support: queue, priority, action, contains PII; SKU: category, publishing status, contradiction).

The reader model was checked on 100 known fields (20 JSON answers rewritten as plain text) and read all of them correctly.

Where the JSON-schema answers missed (grouped over repeats): Haiku 5.5 on tkt-01: auto_action route_specialist instead of auto_replace (1/3 runs); Haiku 5.5 on tkt-01: queue billing_payouts instead of replacements (1/3 runs); Haiku 5.5 on tkt-02: auto_action escalate_security instead of hold_and_audit (2/3 runs); Haiku 5.5 on tkt-02: priority P0_CRITICAL instead of P1_HIGH (2/3 runs).

### 2. Generative synthesis & drafting (per 1k documents, n=12 drafts per cell)

Source: `backend/experiments/cat2_not_stated/drafts.jsonl`, the latest ok draft per document × model × version × repeat: 48 of 48 planned drafts (4 documents × 3 repeats per cell). Costs are recomputed here from each draft's tokens (within $0.0000 per 1k of the summary). ## Known limits of the current data and app

1. **Small samples.** Category 1 has 12 items (36 runs per cell: 12 items × 3 repeats); Category 2 has 4 documents (12 drafts per cell: 4 × 3 repeats); Category 3 has 6 sessions per version (2 scenarios × 3 repeats); Category 4 has 24 answers per version and corpus size (8 questions × 3 sessions); Category 5 has 12 invoices (36 runs per cell: 12 invoices × 3 repeats); Category 6 has 15 runs per cell (3 incidents × 5 repeats). Treat percentages as indicative, not statistically robust.
2. **One reader or judge, one pass.** Category 1 write-ups are read by one reader model (`claude-opus-5-5`, not a model under test) in one pass; it read all 100 known fields of 20 JSON answers rewritten as plain text correctly, but no second reader or human cross-checks it. Category 2 drafts are graded by one blind judge (`claude-opus-5-5`, not a model under test): one rubric pass per draft and head-to-head comparisons shown in both orders, plus a deterministic fact key; no second judge or human grading cross-checks the judge's taste. Category 4 answers get one blind-grader pass each (`claude-opus-5-5`) plus a deterministic answer key; grader noise is not measured and no human graded the answers.
3. **Category 1 ground truth covers 42 fields per repeat** (4 per ticket, 3 per SKU); the other schema fields (reason code, PII categories, hazmat class, material) are not scored. The write-up prompt never lists the allowed labels, so a write-up's score counts whether one allowed label can be read off it (usable as a classification), not the rest of its content. Half the items (the six batch items) are short, so pooled token ranges mix two item sizes.
4. **Category 2 owner and time-window check.** A name or date counts as in the source when it occurs there; the check does not test that the source assigns that action to that owner or date (the judge's errors cover attribution).
5. **Category 3 quality** is a fixed answer key scored by deterministic string matching (no LLM judge). It checks whether specific IDs, amounts and dates survive, not whether an answer is helpful; the recall questions are 6 per scenario (12 in all), each asked once per session.
6. **Extended thinking varies output cost (Category 3).** Calls request `thinking={"type": "between_tools"}`, yet 57 of 192 turn and probe calls in the experiment emitted thinking tokens (up to 3,454 in one call), billed as output; 0 of 18 compactions did. The same turn's output cost therefore varies between repeats, and the per-turn output range above includes thinking.
7. **Category 4 corpus and retrieval.** 8 questions on one deal room; the larger corpus adds two synthetic look-alike suppliers generated by a deterministic transform of Deccan's documents, not real contracts. The clause boundaries and headings Haiku picks from were written for these documents and the clause-pick prompt names the kinds of clause the questions ask about, so the share of needed clauses among its picks may be lower for unseen questions.
8. **Levers changed together.** Category 1's with-design asks for the classification instead of a write-up and enforces the JSON schema in one step, so the saving cannot be split between the two. Category 5's with-design changes the step budget, the policy tool, the data access (one narrow query) and caching together, so its saving cannot be split between them.
9. **Ceiling effects.** Category 5: every recorded cell scored at or near 100% exact vouchers, so accuracy differences between those cells cannot be detected; the invoices test simple arithmetic (quantities × rates, a GST split, a debit note). Category 6: every cell verified 15/15, so these incidents are too easy to show a quality difference with or without the lever, and CpO equals cost per run in every cell.
10. **Cache warming across rungs.** In captures where rungs and items ran in sequence, later calls can read caches written by earlier ones; the *billed* column includes this and the *cold* column removes it. Each Category 6 run writes its own cache (a per-run nonce in the cached prefix).
11. **Trace `finops` is modelled, not billed, for multi-step rollups.** The Category 5 and 6 session rollups store a modelled cold-cache cost (`_compute_step_cold_warm`), not the cache tokens the API returned. `summarize_traces()` averages every trace file per partition without de-duplication. This report recomputes every cost from the recorded tokens instead.
12. **Scale claims.** The deck's daily-volume claim (Category 6: 1.5M–10M tokens/dev/day) and its production-scale framing can't be checked from single runs.
